What we found

The full list, ranked

Most break-ins rely on the same handful of mistakes. Here's what we found on your site, worst first, each with what it means and how to close it.

MED

No shield against injected scripts

Like leaving the upstairs windows unlocked — anything that climbs in can walk around freely.

Your site doesn't tell the browser which scripts are allowed to run. A script slipped in through a plugin flaw or a third-party tag would execute on your pages — including your quote and contact forms.

The fixAdd a Content-Security-Policy. Roll it out in report-only mode first so nothing breaks, then switch it on.
LOW

Your pages can be framed by a fake site

Like someone taping a photo of your storefront over your real front door.

Without this setting, an attacker can load your site inside their own page and trick visitors into clicking things they can't see.

The fixSend an X-Frame-Options (or CSP frame-ancestors) header so other sites can't embed yours.
LOW

Secure connection isn't strictly enforced

Like a good lock on a door that can still be nudged open on the very first push.

The padlock works, but a visitor's first request can be downgraded to an unencrypted connection on shared Wi-Fi before the secure one kicks in.

The fixAdd a Strict-Transport-Security header so every visit is forced onto HTTPS.
INFO

Visitor trails can leak to other sites

Like handing a stranger a note of exactly where your customer just came from.

Internal page addresses can be passed along to third-party sites as visitors click outbound links.

The fixSet a Referrer-Policy header to limit what's shared.
INFO

Browser features left switched on

Like leaving every appliance plugged in and running when you only use one.

Camera, location and other browser features aren't restricted, widening what a malicious script could try to reach.

The fixAdd a Permissions-Policy header that only allows the features you actually use.
INFO

Your admin door opens onto the public street

Like a shop's back-office door opening straight onto a busy sidewalk.

Your WordPress login page is reachable by anyone on the internet, so automated bots can sit there trying password after password.

The fixAdd login rate-limiting and two-factor sign-in, and consider moving the login to a non-obvious address.
INFO

A form endpoint worth double-checking

Like a mail slot you should make sure only accepts letters, not anything shoved through it.

This address accepts input (such as a name and an attachment). It's worth confirming it rejects anything unexpected and limits file uploads. Affected: /api/me, /api/feedback.

The fixValidate and sanitise the inputs, and cap file type and size on any upload.
INFO

Look-alike email from your subdomains

Like someone printing letterhead with your address on it and mailing it out.

Your main domain is protected against spoofing, but mail sent from your subdomains isn't — so it can be forged in your name.

The fixTighten your DMARC record so subdomains are covered by the same quarantine policy.
